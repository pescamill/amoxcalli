## (b1) UNAM · Gran Diccionario Náhuatl and Tlachia

> **Before sending:** the Spanish letter [unam-gdn-tlachia.md](../docs/permisos/unam-gdn-tlachia.md) still says “an index of 13 plates”; since 16 October the bundled catalogue holds **21 plates and 480 occurrences**. Update the letter first.

**To:** Coordination of the Gran Diccionario Náhuatl and the Tlachia project · Instituto de Investigaciones Bibliográficas, UNAM [contact listed on the credits page]
**Subject:** Request for permission to use GDN entries and Tlachia records, with attribution, in an educational platform

Dear coordinators,

I am writing to ask for your explicit permission regarding how **Amoxcalli**, a free and open tool for learning to read Classical Nahuatl, uses the Gran Diccionario Náhuatl and Tlachia.

Before asking, we incorporated in good faith and with attribution **549 Molina entries** taken from the GDN’s public lookup pages (each with a link and the hash of the page consulted; the full dictionary was not downloaded) and an index of **21 Tlachia plates** with their readings and elements. Images load from your site; we do not copy them. None of this is used to train models. If any of these uses seems inappropriate to you, we will remove it immediately and confirm in writing.

**Our request:** permission to link to, keep and incorporate GDN entries and Tlachia records, with the credit you specify, labelled as “automatic extraction” until reviewed. The attached letter (in Spanish) details each use —linking, incorporation, a local copy of images for offline use only— and what we offer in return.

We would be glad to show you the platform in a short session or send you the index for review.

## (b2) *Digital Florentine Codex* (Getty Research Institute)

**To:** Editorial team, *Digital Florentine Codex* [contact listed on the site]
**Subject:** Request for permission to link to and quote the *Digital Florentine Codex*, with attribution, in an educational platform

Dear editors,

I am developing **Amoxcalli**, a free and open tool for learning to read Classical Nahuatl in which every analysis and every explanation shows its source; without evidence, the app does not answer. It runs on the learner’s own computer, with no account and no language model.

Today we only link to your edition from one bibliographic record (Book 11, f. 187v). We have not copied any transcription, translation or image from your site.

**Our request:** permission to link to specific folios of your edition, and to show brief quotations of the Nahuatl transcription and its translation next to a guided reading, with the credit you specify —including the Nahua collaborators and IDIEZ— and a link to the folio. Model training is excluded unless you authorise it in writing; we do not ask for commercial use; and we will remove any content at your request. Images of the manuscript would keep loading from your site, credited to the Biblioteca Medicea Laurenziana as you indicate. The attached letter (in Spanish) gives the details and what we offer in return; I can send an English translation if helpful.

We would be glad to show you the platform or send a sample of how the quotations would look. Thank you for the work that makes the Florentine Codex accessible.

Kind regards,
[name] · [email] · github.com/pescamill/amoxcalli
